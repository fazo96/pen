# Cadence

Cadence reads the sound of prose: how each word is stressed, the rhythm the stresses make, the sounds that echo, how sentences end and begin. It's for near-verse prose, written by ear, by a writer who may not have one for English. It needs no AI; with AI on, Construct reads it too, and can be asked from the panel ([below](#construct)).

## The panel

`components/Cadence.tsx` reads the manuscript's paragraph at the cursor, or the paragraphs a selection touches (`passageAt` in `lib/cadenceText.ts`: up to `MAX_BLOCKS`, as the grammar check sees them, joined by blank lines, mapped back to document positions), again 200 ms after each transaction settles. Each word shows its marks under it (`/` stressed, `x` light, `\` half; dotted when the dictionary gives another stress); a run's syllables get a band (rising, falling, dashed when steady). A tap on a mark tries the other stress (`flips`, kept for that text only, "Undo what-ifs"); a tap on a word opens `CadenceWord`: its IPA (`ipa` in `phones.ts`), where its pronunciation came from, its other stresses to try, Show in text, and the form to say it the writer's way (stress per syllable, sounds-like). Below: the runs, the sound echoes and the sentences, each lighting its words in the passage when picked, the repeated openings, and the guessed words to set. Every term carries a line saying what it means.

Where it shows: from ≥1180px in the Codex entry's slot (`CadencePanel`, an `aside.codex-panel.cadence-panel`, so it takes the panel's layout; it doesn't fade while typing). One at a time: opening an entry closes Cadence (`openEntry` in `Pen.tsx`) and opening Cadence saves and closes the entry; below 2000px either makes way for Construct (`closeSlot`). Open is remembered per device (localStorage `pen:cadence`): it opens again on loading (unless `?entry=` brought an entry) and when the window is wide again. Narrower, the top bar's button and the palette's Show Cadence open the drawer's Cadence tab (`CadenceView`). Not on a Codex entry's own page.

`lib/cadenceClient.ts` holds the page's dictionary (fetched and parsed once, when Cadence first opens) and the writer's words, shared by the panel, the tab and `/settings` (`components/CadenceSettings.tsx`: the words, each removable, and the CMU credit; it loads the words alone, `useCadence({ ownOnly: true })`). A change shows at once and is saved behind; the server's answer wins.

## The analyser

`lib/prosody/` is pure (no server, no DOM) and unit-tested (`tests/prosody.test.ts`), so the page and Construct read a passage the same way: `analyze(text, dict, flips?)` in `index.ts` returns its words, syllables, rhythmic runs, sounds, sentences and openings. A `Dict` is a `lookup` (lowercased word → its pronunciations) and the writer's own words.

- `words.ts`: the words (letters with apostrophes inside; a hyphen splits), what follows each (a clause's end: comma, dash, colon, bracket, a quote; a sentence's: `.` `!` `?` `…`, unless a lowercase word follows or it's "Mr.", and always a line break: a paragraph's end), and how each is said. Looked up in order: the writer's word, the dictionary, a known word plus an ending (`'s`, `-s`, `-ed`, `-ing`, `-ly`, `-er`, `-est`, `-ness`, `-less`, `-ful`, with the base's spelling changes), two known words (way + stone, the second's stress made secondary), else a guess from the spelling (vowel groups less a silent e, the first stressed, no phonemes). Then in context: one-syllable function words (`FUNCTION_WORDS`: articles, prepositions, conjunctions, pronouns, auxiliaries, "there") are light, other one-syllable words stressed; longer words keep the dictionary's stress, and their other pronunciations' stress is kept as `alternatives` ("record"). `flips` replaces a word's marks, for trying another stress.
- `phones.ts`: ARPAbet helpers: syllables (consonants between vowels split by the longest onset English allows), marks (`/` stressed, `\` secondary, `x` light, `?` not known), what a word rhymes on, `soundName` for showing a phoneme to someone who doesn't read ARPAbet.
- `rhythm.ts`: each syllable as a beat; a secondary stress is stressed unless a stress is next to it. A **run** is a stretch of a sentence (it may cross commas, not a `?`) where every stress is one or two light syllables from the next, with up to two light syllables before and after, at least `MIN_FEET` stresses; named iambic, trochaic, anapestic, dactylic, or rising/falling when the feet mix. One kind of foot held for `STEADY_FEET` is `steady`: it starts to sound like verse.
- `sounds.ts`: alliteration (the first consonant of the word or of its stressed syllable), assonance (the stressed vowel, three words at least), rhyme (the stressed vowel and what follows), among words close enough to be heard together (`RULES`), by phoneme, never by spelling. Light words and guessed ones take no part; a group needs two different words. A rhyme between words that both end a clause is `atEnds`.
- `cadence.ts`: each sentence's syllables and ending (firm on a stress, falling on a light syllable, double on two stresses), and sentences opening on the same two or three words, or the same word if it isn't a light one.

## The dictionary

The CMU Pronouncing Dictionary (BSD 2-clause; about 126k words, American): `lib/cmudictBuild.ts` downloads `cmudict.dict` from a pinned commit, checks its sha256, and writes it compact (`word\tPHONES|PHONES`, `parseCompact` reads it) and gzipped to `cache/cmudict/cmudict-<commit>.txt.gz` (0.9 MB), on the first request or ahead with `npm run dictionary` (with WordNet). `lib/pronunciations.ts` holds it gzipped in memory; after a failed download it waits a minute before trying again. `PEN_PRONUNCIATIONS` names a `cmudict.dict`-style file to use instead (the browser tests use `tests/fixtures/cmudict-mini.dict`). `GET /api/pronunciations` serves it gzipped as it is, with an ETag the browser revalidates on each load (304); 503 if it can't be had. Parsing it whole takes about 0.3 s; analysing a paragraph a few milliseconds.

## The writer's pronunciations

For words the dictionary doesn't know (invented names) or says otherwise: `PEN_DIR/.pen-prosody.json`, library-wide and in exports, `{ words: { kvothe: { stress: "/", like: "quoth" } } }`. `stress` is a mark per syllable; `like` is English words it sounds like, whose phonemes it takes (so it joins sound echoes), with the writer's stress over them when the syllables match. `lib/prosody/own.ts` is pure (sanitizing, patches); `lib/prosodyStore.ts` the file, for the route and Construct's tools alike. `GET /api/prosody`; `PATCH /api/prosody` with `{ set: { word, stress?, like? } }` (replaces the word's whole) or `{ remove: word }`. Words are matched lowercased, curly apostrophes straightened; at most 5000, the oldest set dropped first.

## Construct

A language model can't count syllables or hear stress reliably, so Construct gets Cadence's reading rather than its own. `lib/prosody/report.ts` writes a reading as text (`passageReport`: each word with its marks, then runs, echoes, sentence endings, guessed words and the writer's; `SCAN_LEGEND` says how to read it), for the tool and the panel's questions alike.

- Tools (`lib/construct/tools/cadence.ts`, [Construct](construct.md#tools)): `scan_prose` reads the manuscript's paragraphs by heading or lines (headings left out, each cited `pen:L…`, as `grammar_check` finds them: `lib/construct/proseBlocks.ts`; 25 at a time, then where to go on) or any `text` (up to 4000 characters), so Construct checks a rewrite before offering it; book chats only. On the server the dictionary is parsed on first use and kept (`serverDict` in `lib/pronunciations.ts`, about 18 MB), with the writer's words read at each call. `list_pronunciations`, `set_pronunciation` and `remove_pronunciation` do what the word card does, through the same store, in every chat (the Global Codex's too). After a change the session sends a `prosody` event and the page's `cadence.reloadOwn()` takes it, so an open panel follows. The system prompts say to scan before speaking of rhythm, to scan its own rewrites, and to set a pronunciation only when the writer says how a word is said or asks.
- From the panel (AI on: `Pen` passes `ask`): **Explain how it sounds** (beside the passage's syllable count) and **Rewrite for rhythm** (under a sentence picked in Sentences). Both are one-off quick answers (`lib/useQuickAnswer.ts`, shared with the popover over the text, `POST …/construct/quick`, questions up to `MAX_QUESTION`), with Cadence's reading in the question (`lib/cadenceAsk.ts`: `explainPrompt`, `rewritePrompt`, which say the writer asked for rewrites; the quick prompt allows rewrites only then). The answer shows in the panel (`components/CadenceAsk.tsx`) until closed, whatever the cursor does: an explanation as markdown; rewrites read back (`rewritesIn`: numbered lines, a note after " — ", three at most), each scanned by Cadence under the writer's sentence (marks, runs, ending), with **Use this**, which replaces the sentence (checked unchanged first) in one transaction. **Continue in Construct** sends the question to the chat, which can scan with its tools.
